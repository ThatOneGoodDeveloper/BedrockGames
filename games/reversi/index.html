Reversi Unblocked — game guide

Reversi, also known as Othello, looks like a simple game about flipping discs. It is really a game about position. A move can flip a whole line, but the wrong move can hand the opponent a corner and a permanent foothold on the board.

This version implements the full rule set: you must flip at least one line to move, a player with no legal move passes automatically, and the game ends when neither side can move. Legal squares are highlighted, the last move is marked, and the disc count updates after every flip.

The computer uses a positional evaluation that values corners, edges and mobility over raw disc count. That is the same lesson human players need to learn: early disc leads are often a trap, and the player with more options usually wins the endgame.

How to play Reversi

  1. Black moves first. Click any highlighted square to place a disc.
  2. Every disc between your new piece and an existing piece of yours flips color.
  3. If you have no legal move, your turn is skipped automatically.
  4. The game ends when neither player can move.
  5. The player with the most discs at the end wins.

Tips and strategy

  • Corners cannot be flipped — take them whenever it is safe.
  • Avoid playing next to an empty corner unless you have a plan.
  • Mobility matters more than the current disc count.
  • In the endgame, count exact discs and force the opponent into passes.

Reversi FAQ

Is Reversi the same as Othello?

The rules are effectively the same. Othello is a trademarked name for the same game; this page uses Reversi to keep the title generic.

Can two people play on one device?

Yes. Choose two-player mode and share the keyboard, mouse or touchscreen. Black starts.

What happens if I have no legal moves?

Your turn is passed automatically. If neither player has a legal move, the game ends and the discs are counted.

Can I play Reversi unblocked at school?

Yes. It is a static HTML5 game with no downloads or accounts, built for Chromebooks and school networks.
